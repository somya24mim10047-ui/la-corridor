# Western Avenue Commercial Corridor Analysis

## 1. Objective
The objective is to identify and analyze business clusters along Western Avenue in Los Angeles using business location data, road geometry, and spatial clustering.

## 2. Data Sources
- Los Angeles business listing dataset.
- Western Avenue centerline geometry from OpenStreetMap or another verified road-data source.
- Business categories represented by NAICS descriptions.

## 3. Data Preparation
Business records containing longitude and latitude were collected and checked. Duplicate coordinates were examined because multiple businesses may share a building address or geocoded point. Coordinates were projected into UTM Zone 11N (EPSG:32611) for distance calculations in metres.

## 4. Clustering Methods
Two density-based methods were compared:
- DBSCAN: tested with a 40-metre neighborhood radius and a minimum of four points.
- HDBSCAN: tested with a minimum cluster size of four and minimum samples of four.

Both methods were evaluated using unique coordinate locations to reduce the effect of repeated business coordinates.

## 5. Preliminary Results
- Business records analyzed: 251.
- Unique coordinate locations: 75.
- DBSCAN on unique locations: 4 clusters and 7 noise locations.
- HDBSCAN on unique locations: 4 clusters and 6 noise locations.
- Unique locations within 50 metres of the Western Avenue centerline: 72 out of 75 (96%).

## 6. Validation
Road alignment was assessed by calculating the distance between each unique business location and the road centerline. Cluster sizes, noise points, road distances, and business categories were also examined.

Road proximity is a useful validation measure, but it does not independently establish that clusters form a continuous commercial corridor. Map inspection, endpoint selection, and planning evidence are still required.

## 7. Limitations
- Several businesses share identical coordinates, potentially representing building-level geocoding.
- The current dataset covers only the collected geographic area and may not represent the entire Western Avenue corridor.
- Density-based clusters are analytical groupings, not automatically official corridor boundaries.
- Final corridor endpoints and width require documented geographic and planning justification.

## 8. Conclusion
The preliminary analysis identifies spatial groups of businesses near Western Avenue. DBSCAN and HDBSCAN provide comparable cluster counts on unique coordinate locations, while road-distance validation indicates that most unique locations are close to the centerline. Further visual and planning-based validation is necessary before presenting a final corridor boundary as definitive.
